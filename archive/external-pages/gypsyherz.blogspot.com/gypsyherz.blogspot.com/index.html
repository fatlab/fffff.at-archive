Playlists Vs Life

I recently finished making my going away playlist for Berlin, a follow up of sorts to the mix I made for my friends last summer before coming here. As I was making it I couldn't help but notice how different the two are in terms of musical style and general feeling. With one week left here I am feeling all of the emotions that have been building up over the last month, and have to constantly remind myself of the reasons for and importance of going back to Seattle. I am going back to America, going back to Seattle. I refuse, however, to say that I am going home.

Monday, May 17, 2010

Berlin Zoo

Last Monday Dominik and I went to the Zoo to see all the animals. It was quite fun getting to act like kids with no one around to judge us - they were all scared off by the overcast weather! And while I do find it sad that the animals are there in small confined spaces, they are unbelievably pretty and awesome to see.



Hippos! My favorite!!





NOTE: The whole time I couldn't help but think, "Man you'd think in such a capitol city they could have a little more room." While it was neat that the Zoo continued over the Spree and into Tiergarten, I looked it up later and was rather disappointed in Berlin. Seattle's Woodland Park Zoo has 92 acres (.0.37 km²), 1,098 animals from 300 species while Berlin has 86 acres (0.35 km²), 15,912 animals from 1,468 species. Something about that just seems a little off balance to me.

1. Mai Nazifrei

May 1st is probably one of my favorite holidays in Berlin (up there with St Nicholas Day, because who doesn't like waking up with goodies in their shoes). I waited years to have a May Day in Berlin and it was definitely worth it. In theory, and according to Wikipedia, so I guess we know it's the truth, it is something like this:

Today, Berlin witnesses yearly demonstrations on May Day, the largest organized by labour unions, political parties and others by the radical left and Autonomen.

Since 1987, it has also become known for heavy riots in some districts of Berlin. After police action against the radical leftist block in that year's annual demonstrations, the Autonome scattered and sought cover at the ongoing annual street fair in Kreuzberg. (Three years prior to the reunification of Germany, violent protests would only take place in the former West Berlin.)


Though we celebrated with a big breakfast at Maurice's, roaming our Kreuzberg streets and the festivals, buying beer from some people's living room (in attempt to decrease violence, stores aren't allowed to sell alcohol), and settling at a rave around 3 or 4 in the afternoon in Gorlitzer Park to dance (and drink) the sun so sleep. It was perfect just being a few minutes from home for food and bathroom breaks. Other Erste Mai experiences here...
